# File Transfer benchmark, 2026-11-28

Real `rtc/lanes.ts` + `engine/engine.ts` between two headless Edge processes on one machine (AMD Ryzen 9 5900X 12-Core Processor, win32).
Impaired links go through `proxy.ts` (Node UDP proxy: delay, loss, 1 Gbit/s token bucket). The spike measured the
proxy's own ceiling at ~32 MB/s; on this machine it now carries up to ~65 MB/s, and figures near that are its ceiling. Sources are in-memory virtual files
(no disk reads). MB/s = 10^6 bytes/s persisted on the receiver over 10 s after a 3 s warm-up.

- **unordered**: folder/test scheduling (32 MiB ranges, ≤ 2 lanes per file) into a discarding sink.
- **zip-ordered**: zip-stream scheduling (1 MiB ranges, 64 MiB reorder window) into `ZipWriter` (CRC32, headers);
  the archive is dropped instead of streamed through the service worker to disk.

Tuning: defaults from `tuning.ts` (chunk 256 KiB, highWater 8 MiB, lowWater 2 MiB, laneWindow 16 MiB, ackEvery 1 MiB, readBlock 4 MiB). Run time 12.3 min.

## Findings

- **Direct, same machine:** 55–57 MB/s whatever the lane count, as in the spike (47–56). Zip-ordered keeps up
  from 4 lanes on; on 1 lane it loses about a third to CRC32 and the reorder path sharing one thread.
- **Several lanes are what makes long links work.** At 50 ms, 6 lanes reach the proxy's ceiling (64–66 MB/s);
  at 100 ms, 6–8 lanes give 20–34 MB/s; 4 lanes give 14–20 MB/s at 50–100 ms.
- **Single-lane runs through the proxy stall** (0.0–0.3 MB/s, most seconds without progress) at 20–100 ms, except
  one 20 ms zip run (46 MB/s). The proxy counts no drops or losses, so packets are lost between it and Chrome's
  UDP sockets during the first burst, and the one SCTP association backs off for many seconds. The spike measured
  5.4 MB/s for 1 lane at 50 ms. Treat 1-lane proxied figures as a harness artifact; striping hides such stalls,
  which is one more reason for 6 lanes.
- **Zip-ordered now matches unordered** within the noise. Before hedging (`tuning.hedgeMs`, §7.4 of the spec),
  one stalled lane froze the whole zip stream: 6 lanes at 100 ms measured 0.0 MB/s for 30 s.
- **0.1% loss at 50 ms:** 0.8 MB/s on 1 lane, 5.3 / 3.5 on 6 lanes, 7.4 / 5.7 on 8 (unordered / zip-ordered),
  close to the spike (0.9, 6.9 on 8).
- Not measured here: OPFS or real-folder writes (the spike's figures stand), the service worker's path to disk,
  and a real long-distance link between two machines (still to do before tuning further).
